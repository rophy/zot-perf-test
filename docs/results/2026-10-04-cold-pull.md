# Cold pulls — zot vs Distribution vs Harbor

Date: 2026-10-04 · Summary report: [`edge-cache-cold-pull-report.md`](edge-cache-cold-pull-report.md) · Design: [`cold-pull spec`](../superpowers/specs/2026-10-04-cold-pull-design.md) · Step log: [`steps-log.md`](2026-10-04-cold-pull/steps-log.md) · Raw runs: [`runs/`](2026-10-04-cold-pull/runs/)

Question: what does it cost, in CPU, memory and upstream traffic, when the registry has to **fetch, verify, store and serve** an image it has never seen? The upstream is local and unthrottled, so this measures software cost, not network behaviour.

## Setup

- **VM:** same as the warm comparison: multipass, Ubuntu 24.04, **4 vCPU (unpinned), 8 GiB**, on the i7-13620H dev host. One registry runs at a time.
- **Registries:** zot v2.1.21 (on-demand sync, `preserveDigest`, dedupe off, GC off) · Distribution v3.1.2 in proxy mode · Harbor v2.15.2 with a proxy-cache project. Plain HTTP.
- **Upstream:** `registry:2` on the host, holding synthetic single-arch images with random (incompressible) layers, so nothing compresses or dedupes by content.

| Class | Images | Layers | Image size | Used for |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| c10m | 400 | 4 × 2.5 MB | 10 MB | Scenario A |
| c100m | 40 | 4 × 25 MB | 100 MB | Scenario A |
| s100m | 16 | 4 × 25 MB | 100 MB | Scenario B, 1 image per run |
| s1g | 8 | 8 × 128 MB | 1 GB | Scenario B, 1 image per run |

- **Scenario A (unique pulls):** k6 pulls the whole class pool once, every image never seen before, by digest; VUs take the next image from a shared counter. VUs raised 1, 2, 4, 8, … until throughput stopped rising by 5% (never because of CPU). Each step starts from a VM restored to a snapshot with empty caches.
- **Scenario B (stampede):** N = 1, 4, 16, 64 clients start together and pull the **same** cold image. N = 16 and 64 run twice (rows marked run 1 / 2). Each run uses a fresh image; the VM is restored once per registry.
- **Pull model:** as in the warm tests (manifest, then config and layers, 3 in parallel, `Content-Length` checked, bearer token for Harbor); request timeout 600 s; no retries.
- **Measurement window:** from k6 start until upstream transmit and VM disk writes have both stayed below 1 MB/s for 10 s (cap 15 min after k6 end). It is needed because the registries do cold work at different times: zot syncs before it answers, Distribution writes while streaming, Harbor caches in background goroutines after answering. CPU-seconds, memory and disk are over this window; **k6 s** is the client-side duration.
- **CPU** = busy VM CPU-seconds excluding idle, steal **and iowait**; iowait is listed separately. (The first Scenario A pass counted iowait as busy and overstated CPU; all Scenario A rows were recomputed from Prometheus after the fix, and only the corrected numbers are published.)
- The 10 s quiet window usually ends before Harbor's deferred manifest push (it fires at least 20 s after the request), so part of Harbor's caching falls outside the window. The CPU impact is negligible (the push is a small manifest PUT).
- **dedup ×** = upstream blob bytes ÷ image size; 1.00 means one upstream copy.
- **Integrity (Scenario B):** after the quiet window the upstream is stopped and the image is pulled once more; it must succeed.
- 55 steps in total: 19 in Scenario A, 36 in Scenario B (`steps-log.md`). Host CPU during the runs 17–61% (k6 plus the upstream).

## Findings

1. **Cold CPU cost per GB is stable across VU steps for zot and Distribution (within ±10%); Harbor varies (Scenario A).** zot 9.2–9.5 CPU-s/GB (c10m) and 5.9–6.3 (c100m); Distribution 13.5–15.8 and 5.8–6.6; Harbor 58.9–83.7 and 11.0–16.0 (c10m, c100m). Against the warm cost on the same VM (10MB class: zot ≈ 0.9, Distribution ≈ 2.0, Harbor ≈ 11 vCPU per GB/s, i.e. CPU-s/GB), cold filling of small images costs about **10× (zot), 7× (Distribution) and 5–8× (Harbor)** as much per GB. Large images are cheaper per byte: c100m is about 6.5× warm for zot, 3× for Distribution and 1–1.5× for Harbor. Warm reference is the 10MB class only; the 100MB warm cost is 10–30% lower per byte on zot in the local pinned per-thread runs (report §3.2, not the VM). Warm and cold CPU definitions differ slightly: the warm VM figures count iowait as busy, the cold figures exclude it. Warm iowait was 0.1–0.5% of busy CPU at saturation (Prometheus `node_cpu_seconds_total{mode="iowait"}` over the run window: zot 32 VUs 0.08 of 77.8 CPU-s, Distribution 16 VUs 0.13 of 131.8, Harbor 8 VUs 0.74 of 158.2), so the ratios are unaffected.
2. **Scenario A throughput was disk/host-I/O bound, not CPU bound, and must not be read as a registry ranking.** VM cores stayed below 2 in every step (max 1.81; 17 of 19 steps below 1.5), iowait is large and variable (7–300 s per step), and MB/s is erratic (16–250) on a shared host: e.g. Distribution c100m 2 VUs reached 250 MB/s while zot c100m never exceeded 55 MB/s. The ladders ended on the "< 5% gain" rule, never on CPU.
3. **Memory.** In Scenario A zot peaked at 625–666 MB and Distribution at 577–611 MB (VM used, including the OS). Harbor peaked at 1026–1932 MB on c100m and **3200 / 4987 MB on c10m (1 / 2 VUs)**. In the s1g N = 64 stampede: zot 1137–1138 MB, Distribution 770–797 MB, Harbor 1182–1202 MB.
4. **Upstream deduplication: only zot collapses concurrent cold requests.** zot fetched each image once at every N (dedup 1.00 on all 12 zot stampede rows): on-demand sync is a `singleflight` per repo + reference (`pkg/extensions/sync/on_demand.go`). Distribution and Harbor fetch per request, so upstream traffic grows with N: at N = 64 s1g Distribution 62.1× / 60.1× (61.6–63.6 GB pulled from upstream for a 1 GB image), Harbor 65.2× / 65.2×; at N = 64 s100m Distribution 53.0× / 22.8× and Harbor 65.9× / 65.7×. **Harbor is already 2.00× at N = 1** (both s100m and s1g): the client is served from a stream while a background job copies the blob again (`src/controller/proxy/controller.go:285`). Scenario A shows the same: zot and Distribution 1.00, Harbor 2.00 (1.96 on one c10m step).
5. **Harbor caches the manifest at least 20 s after the request, so an offline pull right after our quiet window can fail; under a 1 GB × 64 stampede the deferred push can give up.** The offline integrity check (upstream stopped right after the quiet window) failed on 8 of 12 Harbor stampede rows (s100m N = 1, 4, 64, 64; s1g N = 1, 16 run 2, 64, 64); zot and Distribution passed on all 12 each. Cause, from Harbor v2.15.2 `src/controller/proxy/manifestcache.go:190-217`: every request starts a background goroutine that sleeps 20 s, waits up to 10 × 20 s for blobs still being copied, then pushes the manifest. By row:
   - **Timing artifact (3 rows):** s100m N = 1, s100m N = 4 and s1g N = 1 had 17–18 s windows, so the verification pull ran before the 20 s sleep elapsed; any single pull would fail the check there (e.g. s100m N = 1: window 17 s, next step started 2 s later).
   - **Ambiguous (s100m N = 64 ×2, 36–37 s windows; s1g N = 16 run 2, 56 s):** inside the 20 s + 10 × 20 s budget, so the push may simply not have run yet.
   - **Push gave up (s1g N = 64 ×2, windows 268–281 s, beyond the 220 s budget):** `core.log` shows repeated `MANIFEST_BLOB_UNKNOWN` push failures (36 for s1g image index 3, run 1; 28 for image index 5, run 2). A later offline re-check found 11 of 12 images pullable; the exception was s1g N = 64 run 1 (`20261004T092545Z`), whose manifest stayed uncached until another pull (1 case observed). After a very large stampede Harbor can therefore stay unpopulated and the next pull goes upstream again.
6. **zot cut off 15 of 64 pulls in the first s1g N = 64 run.** Those 15 clients ended with "unexpected EOF" (counted as timeouts; 50.2 GB served instead of 65.5 GB, k6 114 s). The repeat run had 0 failures (73 s). Cause: zot applies a default http `writeTimeout` of 60 s when `http.writeTimeout` is unset (`pkg/cli/server/root.go:41`), which cuts responses that take longer than 60 s to write, here slow 1 GB transfers under 64-way contention. **Set `http.writeTimeout` explicitly for large images.** Go's write deadline starts when the request is read, so it also bounds a cold manifest GET that blocks during on-demand sync: a cold sync longer than 60 s would fail the client regardless of the ingress. That last point is inferred from `net/http` semantics and was not tested.
7. **Time to last client reflects when each registry starts serving.** zot holds every client until the sync completes, then they download from local disk; Distribution and Harbor stream immediately. For one s1g client (N = 1) zot's pull took 7.4 s (p50) against 2.8 s (Distribution) and 2.3 s (Harbor); with 100 MB images the three are within 376–465 ms. At N = 64 s1g time to last client was 114 s / 73 s (zot runs 1 / 2), 155 s / 133 s (Distribution), 270 s / 257 s (Harbor); at N = 64 s100m 11 s / 8 s, 11 s / 7 s, 25 s / 25 s. Two runs of the same step differ by up to 36% at N = 64 (zot s1g: run 2 was 36% faster, 73 s vs 114 s) and by 5× for zot s1g N = 16 (64 s vs 13 s; iowait 162 s vs 5 s in the slow run), so single-digit differences are noise.
8. **Stampede CPU follows the same ordering as Scenario A.** At N = 64 (s1g) VM CPU-s was zot 118 / 80, Distribution 162 / 142, Harbor 432 / 422, i.e. 1.2–2.4, 2.2–2.5 and 6.4–6.6 CPU-s/GB served. These are per GB **served** to 64 clients, so they are not comparable to Scenario A's per-GB-filled figures; the zot run 1 value is also skewed by the 15 failed pulls.

## Caveats

- Shared, noisy dev host; the VM inflates absolute CPU cost for all three registries, so compare ratios, not absolutes. Throughput (MB/s) in Scenario A is dominated by host disk I/O.
- Single runs per step, except Scenario B N = 16 and 64 (two runs).
- Synthetic, incompressible images from a local upstream: no WAN latency, no throttling, no TLS. Time to first byte over a real WAN is not measured.
- Registry memory is VM used (total − available), so the OS and page cache effects are included; the Harbor figure covers all its containers.
- Harbor offline completeness is judged right after the quiet window; see also the timing analysis in finding 5.
- The Harbor `core.log` counts (`MANIFEST_BLOB_UNKNOWN` 36 / 28) and the later 11 of 12 offline re-check were observed in-session and are not archived in the repo.
- The zot `writeTimeout` and Harbor background-push causes come from source reading plus logs, not from a patched re-run.
